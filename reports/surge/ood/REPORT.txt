OUT-OF-DISTRIBUTION (TEXT) QUERIES WITHOUT EXAMPLE QUERIES
Scripts: standin.py, twonorm.py, leverage.py in this directory (200k BioCLIP-2 sample g0-g3, K = 200, G = 12, numpy,
cores 8-11, 152 s / 12 s / 10 s). No repository was edited.

LIMIT OF THIS WORK. The text embeddings are not on disk: agent2/text_q.json and text_train.json hold prompt strings only;
text_q.npy, the text tower and open_clip are gone. Nothing below was measured on real text queries. "Measured" means
measured on the corpus or on data-only stand-in queries, compared with agent2's reported text statistics.

VIEW ON THE 6x GAP
- Touched items (59% against 10.6%): almost none of it is closable without a query log. Touching is decided before any
  code byte is read, from the cluster table, a-hat and one norm. On the best stand-in the plain level-0 bound (no table,
  no alpha*) excludes 2% of items
  (items: 48%); with the whole tail term removed, which no certificate can do at that price, it would exclude 26%
  (items: 73%). (measured on stand-ins) Log-learned anchors are the only lever seen to move it: 74.8% -> 64.1% touched
  at 0.01% answers. (measured by dataonly)
- Bytes (30 MB against 2.6 MB, 11.5x): data-only, items 1 and 5 below give about 10-17% of text bytes (to about 10x).
  With a query log, items 1-3 and 5 stack to about 25-40% (to 7-9x). (modelled) The rest is intrinsic to per-item certificates at this confidence: a text
  query needs the query-weighted error cut to about a quarter before the bulk separates, an item query needs no code
  bits for most items.
- Undecided counts go as width^2.5 to width^4 for text-like queries at 16-96 B and 0.01% answers (width^1.8 to
  width^3 at 0.1%; measured on stand-ins with modelled deterministic widths), so 1% of width is worth about 3% of
  survivors. Small width gains matter more than they look.

RANKED IDEAS

1. One-sided union bound for exclusions. (sound by a short argument; gain modelled; not in the design)
   Mechanism: a wrong exclusion can only happen to a true match, and a wrong inclusion only to a non-match. So the
   upper bounds used to exclude need a union over (true matches x levels), not N x 5. Lower bounds (used for tau and for
   certifying inclusion) keep the constant for N. For k-NN the count is k. For thresholds use a cap M on the answer size,
   doubling with a halved share of epsilon; Markov on the number of false exclusions covers the case |S| > M.
   Arithmetic: N = 1e9, eps = 1e-9: c = 9.35 now; k = 10 gives c_ex = 7.1 (width x0.76 on the randomised part);
   a 0.01% answer gives 8.4 (x0.90). On the stand-in, scaling the modelled width by 0.76 takes undecided from 19.6% to 8.3% at 32 B and 1.7% to
   0.6% at 96 B. Applied to agent2's level table for text: about -8 to -14% bytes for k-NN, -4 to -7% for thresholds;
   more if the schedule is re-tuned, since the randomised gain goes as sqrt(n)/c.
   Cost per item: none. Data-only: yes. Test: in knn/sim.py use c = 7.1 for upper bounds and 9.35 for lower bounds;
   check zero violations and bytes. The proof needs a proofcheck pass.

2. Gap direction as one exact scalar per item, learned from the query log. (argued; not data-only)
   Mechanism: for any fixed unit g with g.r stored per item, D.r = (D.g)(g.r) + D'.r with D' orthogonal to g; the first
   term is exact, and the certificate uses D' in place of D at every level.
   Arithmetic: the gap holds 17% of text offset energy (agent2, measured), so width x0.91 if it is spread like the rest,
   up to about x0.8 if it sits in the uncoded mid band; with the exponent of 3 that is 25-50% fewer undecided, about
   -10 to -25% bytes after charging the scalar. Item queries gain nothing and pay 1 B.
   Cost: 1 B per item at level 0. The gap is mostly orthogonal to the corpus mean (61-98% of its energy, derived from
   cosine 0.92 and norms 0.76 and 0.31; the text mean norm is not reported), so the corpus cannot supply it; about 100 logged queries can. Test: needs text_q.npy re-encoded; measure
   |WD'|/|WD| over (query, cluster) pairs.

3. Query log at compaction, profile and anchors. (upper bound measured by dataonly)
   Worth exactly the mixture-tuned build: text -8.3 / -5.6 / -2.5 / -1.1% bytes against H25 and touched 75% -> 64%.
   A log-fitted prefix basis is the larger prize (predicted text -24% width at 96 B) but the full version costs items
   +21% (agent2, predicted), so it only fits as a few leading dimensions. 

4. Spectrum-flattened items as the data-only stand-in for out-of-distribution queries. (measured match; small saving)
   q = unit(mu + |x - mu| * unit(L^(-p/2) (x - mu))) in the global PCA basis, p = 0.5 to 0.75.
   Against agent2's text figures (|D| 0.73; band shares 12 / 14 / 12 / 15 / 20 / 23 / 4; score sd 0.052; threshold minus
   median 0.185; nearest-centre cosine 0.75): p = 0.75 gives 0.68; 16 / 15 / 9 / 13 / 18 / 21 / 9; 0.063; 0.140; 0.80.
   Items within +-0.005 / 0.01 / 0.02 of the top-20 threshold per match: 1.9 / 6.2 / 38.6 against text 1.7 / 5.5 / 38.
   Undecided after 16 / 32 B: 39.5% / 19.6% against text 45% / 29%; it under-predicts at 96 B (1.7% against 8.5%).
   Other stand-ins are worse: item differences match the bands but not the distance (|D| 1.19); item plus noise and
   PC-removed items put energy in the wrong bands; random directions are far off.
   As a profile it buys little: modelled width loss for text (a synthetic profile with agent2's band shares) is 1.17 (item profile), 1.056 (H25), 1.018 (H50), 1.013
   (square root of the item profile), against item losses of 1.00 / 1.014 / 1.053 / 1.081. My reconstruction reproduces
   dataonly's 1.14-1.19 and 1.06-1.08, and no candidate beats the H family's trade by more than 2-3%. The profile
   route is exhausted. Its use is as a test workload: it gives every dataset an out-of-distribution cost figure and
   a way to set the hedge weight that does not rest on BioCLIP text. Cost: none. Test: run the 1M harness with it.

5. Free deterministic refinements already known (D orthogonal to the code, alpha*). A few per cent. (argued)

NEGATIVE RESULTS

6. Per-query certificate weights are not free. (proved for one norm; measured for two)
   With one stored norm s = |W^-1 e| the feasible errors are an ellipsoid whose support function in direction D is
   exactly s|WD|; any other diagonal W' gives |W'D| * max_i(W_i/W'_i) * s >= |WD| * s. With the plain norm stored as
   well there is a free one-parameter family, bound^2 = min_k (|e|^2 + k s^2) * sum_i D_i^2 / (1 + k W_i^-2), valid for
   every k. It gains 2-10% over min(plain, weighted) for text-like queries under the item profile, and 0-2% under H25.
   H25 already is that compromise.

7. Query-side subspace split, tail scalars, banded norms. (measured on stand-ins; agrees with agent2)
   Level-0 exclusion for the p = 0.75 stand-in: plain 2.2%, two bands 3.1%, three bands 3.4%. The premise is only
   partly right: text has 3.8% of its offset energy in local dimensions 512-768, which really are dead, but 43% in
   128-512, where residuals still hold 14% of their energy. A sketch of that band needs over 137 bits to match
   Cauchy-Schwarz at c = 9.35 (79 bits at c = 7.1). (argued)

8. Rank rather than bound for k-NN. (argued from knn's measured oracle gap)
   The k-th score is known exactly once the seed is read, so a relative bound is the same bound. A perfect seed is
   worth at most 7% for text (29.9 against 27.8 MB).

CORRECTION TO THE BRIEF'S PICTURE. On stand-ins the clusters still explain about 85% of score variance, as for items;
the margin from threshold to median is 5-7 within-cluster sds against 9 for items. The loss is that Cauchy-Schwarz is
9-15x above the actual |D.r| and the margin is only about that wide. (measured on stand-ins; unverified on text)
