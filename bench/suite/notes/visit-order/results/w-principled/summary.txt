A principled width for the approximate k-NN's deterministic levels: why W2 as built beat the centre-corrected form on
iNaturalist, and what to time instead
====================================================================================================================

Branch research/w-principled off 92afad9b7. Nothing here is timed: the reader ran on cores 0-3 at nice 19 while the
benchmark held core 11. Every count is the real reader's (`Adaptive::knn`) on the suite's fits and prepared queries,
100 queries, k 10. Time is modelled from the counts (see "The cost model").

Short answer
------------
- **Part of the gap in tonight's timing comes from the constants, not the width.** With the constants held equal, the two
  forms make the same stop and path decisions at every psi: the same clusters read, direct, opened, groups and stored
  vectors. The two timing runs each measured their own constants (dir_us 0.61 against 0.50, grp_us 600 against 520).
  That moves clusters between the direct and index paths. It explains all of "11,346 against 8,451 stored reads" and
  about 0.004 of the 0.011 recall gap at psi 5.
- **The rest is the levels.** At psi 5 on iNaturalist, with the same constants:
  - as built: recall 0.985, 7 true neighbours per 1,000 dropped at the deterministic levels;
  - corrected: recall 0.976, 16 dropped;
  - both: 7 unread, and none dropped at the randomised levels.
  The corrected form is the better model of an average row's error. True neighbours are not average rows: in its own
  spreads, a true neighbour's error is its height in the cluster (z0 = 1.0 h on all four datasets). W2 does not model
  that.
- **Widening the levels is almost free; raising psi is not.** At psi 5 on iNaturalist, as built, corrected and the fix
  below all model at 2,418 us. Corrected reaches the same recall only near psi 5.5, at 2,946 us. As built's extra width
  is larger in the clusters that hold neighbours: a median 1.07 times corrected's, 1.13 in those clusters. So it buys
  back neighbours cheaply. On LAION the extra width is about 1.01, which is why the forms tie there.
- **A principled fix exists and is implemented as a knob: the height width.** It is the width that keeps a row scoring
  the running k-th best with probability Phi(psi), with the level's error and the row's deviation modelled as jointly
  Gaussian:
  - w = (sqrt(1 - f) max(0, zeta) + psi sqrt(f)) tau;
  - f = 1 - tau^2 / sigma^2, and zeta = (t - mu) / sigma.
  It needs nothing stored beyond W2. Against corrected it is 0.70 to 0.97 of the modelled time to recall 0.9 to 0.99
  unfiltered on the four datasets. It is level or better on region-25, and level with or better than as built in every
  cell.
- **Recommendation: time `--level-width height` against the default (W2 corrected) on the same index copy**, so that
  the two runs share one set of constants.

2. Why the corrected width drops true neighbours (inat-post.txt, *.nb, nbfit.py, post.py)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
For each true neighbour in an index-path cluster, the study reads that cluster again alone, with the same width and
running k-th best, to find the level that drops it. It also takes the neighbour's height h = (s - mu) / sigma and its
level-0 error over W2's tau, z0 = (s - est0) / tau0, using the interval midpoint at the certified width.

- **A neighbour's error is its height.** Least squares z0 = a h over the neighbours (corrected, psi 9, unfiltered):

  | dataset | neighbours | median h | median z0 | a | MMSE's a, tau/sigma |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | iNaturalist | 265 | 3.38 | 3.26 | 1.01 | 0.28 |
  | LAION | 994 | 3.39 | 3.33 | 1.00 | 0.48 |
  | yahoo | 998 | 3.99 | 3.53 | 0.90 | 0.40 |
  | arxiv | 877 | 4.05 | 4.06 | 1.01 | 0.41 |

  log(z0 / h) does not move with log(tau / sigma) (slope -0.25 to 0.18). In its own spreads, the error of a row at
  height 4 is about 4, whatever share of the cluster's spread the level codes. W2 predicts the spread of an average
  row's error, about 1 in those units.
- **Which neighbours are dropped.** At psi 5, corrected drops neighbours at a median h of 4.2, zeta 4.0 and z0 6.2. Those
  it keeps have a median h of 3.3, zeta 2.8 and z0 3.1. They are the far tail of far clusters.
- **What the bulk shows** (inat-post: every row of every index-path cluster at psi 5, 1,356 clusters):
  - corrected is well calibrated for the bulk: sd(e0) / tau0 is a median 0.88 (0.98 in clusters holding a neighbour);
    as built is wide (0.82);
  - the error's regression on the row's deviation, c = cov(e0, d) / var(d), is 1.96 times the least-squares-predictor
    value (MMSE: (sd_e / sd_d)^2) on iNaturalist, against 1.13 on LAION;
  - corr(e0, est0) is 0.27 on iNaturalist and 0.10 on LAION; MMSE would make it 0.
- **The share of rows at or above the final k-th best, by x0 = (t_fin - est0) / tau0, grows with the cluster's
  zeta_fin** (iNaturalist, psi 5):

  | x0 | zeta < 3 | 3 to 4.5 | >= 4.5 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 4 to 5 | 0 (3,724 rows) | 3.7e-3 (6,411) | 8.2e-3 (855) |
  | 5 to 6 | 0 (6,335) | 7.3e-4 (16,350) | 1.9e-3 (3,116) |
  | 6 to 8 | 0 (20,325) | 3.5e-5 (84,700) | 3.7e-5 (27,303) |

  W2's psi tau keeps a row by x0 alone. The chance that a row near the cut is a neighbour depends on how far above its
  cluster the threshold sits, and that is the term W2 lacks.
- **Where as built's extra width falls.** Median tau_built / tau_corrected over index-path clusters at psi 5:

  | dataset | all clusters | clusters holding a neighbour |
  |---|---|---|
  | iNaturalist | 1.069 | 1.133 |
  | arxiv | 1.087 | 1.139 |
  | yahoo | 1.036 | 1.055 |
  | LAION | 1.010 | 1.016 |

  The centre's term is largest where the query lines up with the cluster's centre, which is where neighbours are. The
  extra width mimics a height term in part, by accident. On LAION it is too small to matter.

3. Why that recall is cheap at the levels and dear through psi (lead 2)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- At one psi every rule makes the same stop and path choices (section 1). A rule that drops fewer neighbours at the
  levels therefore gains recall at the cost of the extra rows past the levels alone.
- Modelled time at psi 5, iNaturalist:
  - corrected, as built and height: 2,419, 2,418 and 2,418 us, at recall 0.976, 0.985 and 0.986;
  - tail (below): 2,424 us at recall 0.992;
  - corrected at psi 5.5: 2,946 us for 0.987.
  Raising psi buys clusters, groups and stored vectors. Widening the levels buys rows through a 16 B level and a few
  more through the randomised levels.
- **A principled level dial from the stop's psi does not come out of W2's own model.** Equating marginal recall per
  unit cost:
  - opening a cluster at the stop's margin gives n_v Phi_bar(psi_s) expected neighbours for C_cl;
  - keeping a row at x gives Phi_bar(x) for c_row;
  - so Phi_bar(psi_l) = kappa Phi_bar(psi_s), with kappa = c_row n_v / C_cl, about 7 to 30 on iNaturalist;
  - that gives psi_l of about 4.3 to 4.5 at psi_s 5, narrower than the stop.
  The data say the opposite. The earlier psi - 1.5 dial lost everywhere, and every wider rule here wins. The Gaussian
  row model misprices the neighbours' tail by orders of magnitude (Phi_bar(5) = 3e-7 against the 7e-4 to 2e-3 measured
  above). So the coupling cannot be derived until the level's error model is selection-aware. Once it is (section 4),
  one psi means a per-neighbour miss rate of about Phi_bar(psi) at both the stop and the levels. Sharing psi is then the
  natural coupling, not an accident.

4. The fixes, derived (`study_width_rule` in rust/src/v2/e2e.rs; the knob in q.rs and wrule.rs)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Notation: tau is W2's (corrected) spread at the level, sigma = sigma_c(q) the cluster's spread along the query (the
order state's), mu its centre score, t the running k-th best, zeta = (t - mu) / sigma, and f = max(0, 1 - tau^2 /
sigma^2) the share of the cluster's score variance along the query that the level explains.

**height** (the recommendation; library knob `LevelWidth::Height`, bindings `level_width = "height"`, ab_orders.py
`--level-width height`).
- Assumptions:
  - a row's deviation d = s - mu and the level's error e are jointly Gaussian over the cluster;
  - the estimate is a least-squares predictor of the score, so its error is uncorrelated with it (MMSE).
- Then cov(e, d) = tau^2, and e given d has mean (1 - f) d and spread sqrt(f) tau.
- A row scoring t is kept with probability Phi(psi) when the width is (1 - f)(t - mu) + psi sqrt(f) tau, that is
  w = (sqrt(1 - f) max(0, zeta) + psi sqrt(f)) tau. zeta is taken at least 0: below the cluster's mean, the threshold
  needs no shift.
- No constant, and nothing stored beyond W2. The reader computes sigma from the coefficients it already holds (r
  products), so the cost is a few flops per index-path cluster.
- It is the earlier sampled-width round's "kappa = 1 - f" width, with W2's tau in place of a sample.
- The assumption is measurably wrong in one direction: the neighbours' errors are about h tau, not sqrt(1 - f) h tau.
  So height under-corrects. It still removes nearly every level drop at psi 5 and above: 6, 3, 2 and 0 per 1,000 on
  iNaturalist, LAION, arxiv and yahoo, against corrected's 16, 20, 21 and 4.

**tail**, the measured law instead of MMSE: w = (max(0, zeta) + psi sqrt(f)) tau.
- The fitted slope a of z0 on h is 0.9 to 1.0, not a chosen constant, but it is an empirical regularity, not a
  derivation.
- It removes every level drop. On iNaturalist it is the best rule found (0.68 to 0.89 of corrected).
- It passes 3 to 4 times height's rows past the deterministic levels on LAION and yahoo, and loses there at recall 0.99
  (1.07 to 1.09 of corrected).

**cross**, the stored cross form, as an oracle: e = c d + noise, with c = cov(e, d) / var(d) taken from every row of
the cluster (what one more r x r form per cluster and level, like W2's M, would supply per query).
- w = (cs max(0, zeta) + psi sqrt(1 - cs^2)) tau, with cs = min(1, c sigma / tau). MMSE's c gives height.
- On iNaturalist it is about height (0.92 / 0.83 / 0.81 / 0.79 of corrected).
- On LAION it is far worse: 48 neighbours per 1,000 dropped at the levels at both psi 5 and 6 (corrected 20 and 5),
  and 1.27 and 3.5 times corrected's time to 0.9 and 0.95; 0.97 and 0.99 are not reached.
- The cause: LAION's own regression is large (c 0.37, cs about 0.7), which cuts the spread term to 0.7 psi tau. The
  neighbours' tail is wider than a linear-Gaussian conditional allows, so a more exact second moment narrows the width
  where it should widen.
- So one more stored form per cluster and level is not worth its 1 KB: the arxiv and yahoo runs are in cross-*.txt.

Also run, as references for the width alone: corrected times 1.2 and 1.5 (a fitted multiplier, which is what this study
was asked to avoid). Times 1.2 tracks height within a few percent everywhere; times 1.5 tracks tail.

6. The running threshold (lead 3)
--------------------------------
- In iNaturalist's index-path clusters at psi 5, (t_fin - t_run) / tau0 is 0 at the median and 0.27 at p90. In the
  clusters holding a neighbour, which are opened earlier, it is 0.37 at the median and 1.7 at p90.
- A neighbour scores at least t_fin, so a running t below t_fin only adds slack. Every rule drops fewer neighbours
  against t_run than it would against t_fin. The running threshold costs rows, never recall, and calls for no change to
  the error model.
- Height takes zeta at t_run, which is the conservative side.

7. The cost model
-----------------
T = a0 + c_vis read + sig spreads + c_open opened + c_byte sum_l enter_l bytes_l + c_full stored + c_grp groups, per
query. bytes_l is each level's code bytes per row (from the code files). The unit costs come from these sources:
- iNaturalist: the W2 phase profile (results/w-rule/profile-*.txt) against this study's counts for the same build and
  constants:
  - c_full = FULL / stored = 0.119, c_vis = (ORDER + CHOICE) / read = 2.6, sig = 2.2;
  - c_open = (CLSETUP + INIT + LVSETUP + TABLES) / opened = 14.8;
  - c_byte = (DECODE + BOUNDS) / byte-rows = 1.19e-4, the same at psi 5 and 6;
  - c_grp = the remainder over groups = 620 (grp_us measured 600).
  It reproduces the profile's mean QUERY (2,585 against 2,587). Against the timed medians it is within 0.86 to 1.15.
- LAION: the same, with the unlisted phases (REPACK, COMPACT) put on opened: c_open 27.8, c_byte 1.55e-4, c_full 0.061,
  c_vis 1.68, sig 0.85, c_grp 34.4. It matches the profile's means within 2%. It is 1.1 to 1.3 times the timed medians
  (mean against median).
- yahoo and arxiv have no W2 profile:
  - c_byte is LAION's;
  - c_full is scaled by bytes from the profiles (yahoo 0.034 from rand-sample's FULL; arxiv 0.092);
  - c_grp and sig are the measured constants;
  - a0 and c_open are fitted to the timed medians of 6f86a1c's run (fitcost.py: yahoo 117 and 13.5, arxiv 88 and 8.2),
    within 0.84 to 1.11.
  Read these two datasets' ratios as rough.
- The rules differ, at one psi, only in rows past level 0 and the stored vectors behind them. So the ratios between
  rules depend mostly on c_byte and c_full, both read straight from the profiles.

8. The knob, its checks, and what stays the same
-----------------------------------------------
- `q::LevelWidth { W2, Height }`, `KnnReader::with_level`, `Adaptive::level` (W2 unless set), `CQ2::set_height`,
  `wrule::height_factor`; bindings: `Index.level_width` ("w2" / "height"); ab_orders.py `--level-width`.
- The library's Height against the study's hook rule (lib-*.txt): every count identical at psi 3 to 7, unfiltered and region-25, on iNaturalist, LAION and arxiv. lib:w2 equals the hook's corrected rule and this study's earlier runs.
- Tests: cargo test --release --no-fail-fast gave 117 passed, 4 failed (the known synth4k four), 15 ignored; the second binary 4 passed. The exact-path hash is f1f482ad11b64d9e and knn2_fast's 90d7892d504ef425, both unchanged. The_statistical_width_drops_only_what_the_certified_width_keeps now covers Height and passes. wrule's the_height_width_is_w2s_at_the_cluster_mean_and_widens_above_it was written after that run and has not been run yet.
- The exact paths and the approximate threshold query do not read the rule.
- Study only: `q::WIDTH_HOOK` (an optional override of the factor of every index-path cluster k-NN opens at a W2 width,
  for these studies); study_w_principled.
