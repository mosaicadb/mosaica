Stored correction: a per-cluster ratio on the approximate k-NN's deterministic level width, timed against d999606 and a351f13
==========================================================================================================================

The build (99b033c, branch research/stored-correction off a351f13)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- At build, `adaptive::level_ratios` measures each cluster's ratio of actual error spread to the derived width at each
  deterministic level. It follows the sample-when-needed replay's "A data cluster ratio" (replay.py):
  - the queries are 400 corpus rows, each left out of its own cluster;
  - each query reads its first 20 clusters by mu + z(1, v) sigma;
  - each row gives z = (s - m) / (rho_g,l h);
  - a cluster's ratio is the root mean square of z over its pairs, or its group's when it has fewer than 3 pairs.
  The ratio is saved as level_ratio.bin (store format 12, in the store hash). A missing file is refused at the open,
  with a request to rebuild, and so is an index without ratios at Adaptive::new.
- k-NN reads each cluster's deterministic levels on the index path at min(1, psi rho_g,l r_c,l) of the upper
  half-width. The randomised levels, the order and the stop are as in a351f13. Exact paths and the threshold query do
  not read the ratio.
- The base read the deterministic levels at min(1, psi rho_l), with rho_l the median of rho over the groups. The
  ratio is measured against each group's own rho, as in the replay, so the new width uses rho_g,l, not the median.
- `level_psi` (Adaptive::knn_levels and the bindings) sets the deterministic levels' psi only. The randomised levels
  stay at the stop's psi, as on research/sampled-width (0a4a00979). ab_orders.py takes --level-psi and
  --level-psi-offset C (level psi max(0, psi - C)).

How it departs from the replay. A build cannot know the benchmark queries, so:
- the 400 rows are drawn uniformly from the index by the build's seed, not from outside the benchmark queries' 100
  nearest;
- the queries are the stored f16 vectors, not corpus32;
- a cluster whose group has no training row would fall back to the whole index's ratio, then to 1 (the replay would
  give it 0). This did not happen on any of the three indexes.

The replay reproduced (ratios-*.log, check_ratios.sh, check_ratios.py)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
- With the replay's own 400 training queries (study_level_ratios), the library's ratios match the replay's per
  cluster to |log| <= 3e-6 at both levels on iNaturalist, LAION and yahoo.
- With the build's own 400 rows, they differ from the replay's by a median |log| of 0.01 to 0.03, p90 0.03 to 0.11.
  Median ratios: iNaturalist 1.31 / 1.29 (levels 0 / 1), LAION 1.09 / 1.07, yahoo 1.03 / 1.01.

Costs
-----
Build, on 8 threads on cores 0-7 at nice 19:
- iNaturalist 5.8 s, of a 156 s build;
- LAION 1.1 s, of 32 s;
- yahoo 0.9 s, of 27 s.

Query: the reader reads the ratio and computes its width per cluster opened on the index path, inside the cluster's
setup time (P_CLSETUP). It takes 4.9 to 5.7 ns per cluster (study_level_ratios), about 60 ns for a query that opens 12
clusters, against a query time of about 2.4 ms. The cost model's constants are timed without a width, as before.

Tests (cargo test --release --no-fail-fast): 114 passed, 4 failed, 10 ignored; the second binary 4 passed.
- The 4 failures are the known synth4k ones (synth4k_reference_hashes, knn2_fast_is_unchanged,
  the_exact_paths_are_unchanged, the_constants_change_the_answer_only_through_a_sigma_and_the_moments).
- Against a351f13 on the same regenerated fixture:
  - every exact-path line (VEXACT_EXACT_LINES, 8,520 lines) is byte-identical;
  - knn2_fast's hash is the same;
  - the code hash is the same;
  - only the store hash moves, because it now covers the ratios.
- New tests:
  - the_level_ratios_are_stored_and_survive_a_reopen;
  - the_statistical_width_drops_only_what_the_certified_width_keeps (over every level, the integer and float levels,
    and psi up to 50; equal to the certified read where the cap binds at every level);
  - the_levels_psi_is_psi_unless_set;
  - a_saved_index_opens_as_the_index_it_saves, which now refuses a missing level_ratio.bin and compares the ratios.

What the runs say
-----------------
- **iNaturalist unfiltered is mostly repaired.** Recall at psi 5 rises from 0.948 to 0.978. Time to recall 0.95 / 0.99
  falls from 1.16 / 1.64 of spread to 0.71 / 1.06. That is level with d999606 at 0.9 and 0.95, but not yet at 0.99
  (1.06 against 0.87).
  - Rows past the deterministic levels at psi 5 fall (184 to 124) even though the width is wider. This is likely
    because the k-th best is higher once the true neighbours are found sooner. Not checked.
- **arxiv unfiltered gains too.** Recall at psi 5 rises from 0.902 to 0.925, and time to recall 0.99 falls from 1.00
  to 0.66 of spread.
- **LAION and yahoo unfiltered stay level with a351f13**, within 0 to 6%, as their ratios (median 1.03 to 1.09)
  predict.
- **The filtered sets are level with a351f13 except in three places.**
  - iNaturalist region-25 at 0.95 is better (0.62 to 0.49).
  - arxiv random-25 at 0.99 is better (0.71 to 0.65).
  - yahoo region-1 is about 1.3 times slower (0.29 to 0.39 at 0.95). The cause is not the width but the path choice.
    The constants timed at this arm's open differ from a351f13's (row_us_16 0.063 against 0.034), so 1.8 clusters
    per query go to the index path where a351f13 read every cluster directly.
  - On random-1 and random-25 nearly every cluster is read directly (0 to 7 rows past the deterministic levels), so
    the width has nothing to act on there.
- **Level psi offset 1.5 is worse everywhere unfiltered.** It lowers recall at every psi (iNaturalist 0.943, arxiv
  0.828 at psi 5). In the spread-normalised columns it takes 1.0 to 2.1 times the default dial's time at a given
  recall. With the stored ratio, the levels want the stop's psi.
- **psi for 0.95 unfiltered** with the stored ratio, interpolated from the recalls above: about 4.3 on iNaturalist,
  5.0 on LAION, 5.4 on arxiv and 5.7 on yahoo. a351f13 needs 5.1 on iNaturalist and 5.7 on arxiv.
- **a351f13 against d999606** compares everything merged since the start of the day, not the soundness fixes alone:
  the pooled stop, the derived width, rotations on first read, the group cost and the soundness fixes. So these runs
  cannot price the soundness fixes on their own. Overall:
  - unfiltered, a351f13 is faster on LAION, yahoo and arxiv, and slower on iNaturalist at high recall (the derived
    width this build corrects);
  - region-25 is much faster everywhere.
