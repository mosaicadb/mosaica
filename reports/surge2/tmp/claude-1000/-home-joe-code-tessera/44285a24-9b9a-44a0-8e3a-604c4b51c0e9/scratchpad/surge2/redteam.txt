RED TEAM: THE EXACT, FILTER-AWARE VECTOR INDEX
2026-11-13. Reading only; nothing run. V = size of the visible-and-filtered set a query runs over.
All "measured" figures are from bench/REPORT.md (glove, nytimes) and rust/REPORT2.md (BioCLIP 1M, n = 768).

VERDICT IN FIVE LINES
- The measured index never beats a plain scan of the visible set on glove or nytimes, at any selectivity
  (0.4x unfiltered, 0.9-1.0x at 50%, 0.6-0.8x at 10%, 0.1-0.3x at 1%, 0.03-0.08x at 0.1%). Every claim that it does is modelled.
- On BioCLIP it wins clearly only for item queries (18-36x over a flat scan). For text, the stated common case,
  it is 2.5x a flat int8 scan for k-NN and about 1.0-1.2x for thresholds.
- Its filtered advantage is over badly configured graph baselines. The real competitor is "pre-filter, then scan",
  which is exact, leaks nothing, and was left out of the comparison as first written.
- What is new and probably real: sound per-item intervals whose k-NN constant does not grow with N (Theorem A),
  and a threshold predicate with set semantics. That is a certified RaBitQ scan, not a new index family.
- It is linear in V. The honest regime is V between about 10^4 and a few 10^7 at 768 dimensions.

1. THE CASE AGAINST, CLAIM BY CLAIM

Exactness. Simplest competitor: a flat SIMD scan (exact, eps = 0, no proof needed), or an int8 or 1-bit scan
with a sound bound and a re-rank (the VA-file, 1998; LEMP). Measured: glove exact 29.9 QPS against flat 75.5;
nytimes 44.6 against 110. BioCLIP: item k-NN 4.8 ms against flat 88 (int8) or 175 (f16): 18-36x. Text k-NN 35.3
ms: 2.5-5x, and only 1.5x against the 54 ms BLAS float scan in knn/REPORT (perhaps another machine). Text thresholds: 75.6 ms (1% answer)
and 110 ms (5%) against 88 ms for flat int8, i.e. 1.2x and 0.8x; on 4 threads 21.0 against 23. So for text
thresholds the whole structure is worth nothing over a flat int8 pass plus a re-rank of the borderline items.

Exact filtering. Competitor: pre-filter plus scan of the visible set. Measured crossover on BioCLIP (random
filter): items win 5.7x at V = 100k (3.5 against 20 ms), tie at V = 10k (1.3 against 1.2), lose 2.8x at V = 1k.
Text: 1.4x at V = 500k (71 against 99), tie at 100k (23 against 21), lose at 10k and below. Glove and nytimes:
lose 3-35x at 1% and 0.1%, tie at 50%. The HNSW rows prove little: hnswlib ran a Python callback per node, faiss
HNSW was one M-16 build that returns short lists. No ACORN, no filtered DiskANN, no native filter functor. A fair
graph baseline at 1-10% selectivity will reach recall 0.95-0.99 at speeds well above ours; whether it reaches
0.999 is unmeasured, and for text even unfiltered HNSW only reached 0.947 at ef 512.

Bytes read. Nobody pays for bytes in RAM; they pay for time. v2 costs 1.4 ns per byte end to end; a flat f16 scan
costs 0.11 ns per byte (175 ms / 1.536 GB) and faiss fast scan about 0.07-0.11. A 50x saving in bytes for text
(2%) is a 2.5-5x saving in time. On disk the unit is pages, not bytes, and DESIGN admits a possible 10-30x page
multiplier, unmeasured. At N = 1e9 the percentages are 2.6 GB per item query and 30 GB per text query; DiskANN
reads about half a megabyte at any N (from memory).

Memory. 39-119 B per item resident. HNSW at 3.2 KB per item is a strawman at this scale. DiskANN keeps 30-60 B per
item (from memory); a 1-bit RaBitQ code at 768 dimensions is about 100 B. Parity, not an advantage. The one real point: once
N x 2n bytes exceeds RAM (about 40M items per 64 GB at 768 dimensions), "scan the visible set" becomes V random
page reads, so some resident certified code is needed for V above about 10k. Any sound code does that job.

Regime map (768 dimensions, one machine, linear extrapolation from 1M on 4 threads; unverified above 1M):

  V                 exact option that wins                         margin
  < 10k             scan of visible (text: < 100k)                 scan wins 1-35x
  10k - 4M          index, items 5-36x; text 1-2.5x                a flat scan is still under 100 ms here
  4M - 34M (items)  index is the only exact answer under 100 ms    2.9 ms per 1M items
  4M - 8M (text)    same, a band of 2x (12.5 ms per 1M)            up to ~40M only if the modelled 1-bit pass holds
  above that        nobody is exact and interactive; ANN, or exact as a batch answer in seconds
  N = 1e9 unfiltered: items 3-5 s, text 12-35 s, all levels in RAM (120 GB). Not a map interaction.

  Dimension: wins grow with n and with spectrum decay. At n = 100 the deterministic cap is 9.95 sd, randomness
  buys 1.66x, and a flat scan is cheap; the measured index loses everywhere. k: cost nearly flat (k = 1 to 100:
  2.7x for items, 1.5x for text), which favours large k and thresholds with large answers, where graphs are weak.
  Selectivity at N = 1e9: the niche is V = 10k to 34M for items (0.001% to 3%) and 100k to 8M for text (0.01% to
  0.8%). Below V = 4M the gain is cores and avoided disk reads, not feasibility.

2. WHAT IS SOLID, WHAT IS MODELLED

Solid: correctness (0 violations in 731M interval checks; 20,000 benchmark queries equal to brute force); every
QPS in bench/REPORT.md and REPORT2.md; the YFCC visible-set distribution (median 14k, sum 18.9e9); the sphere-law
tail check; the negative result that no stored cluster bound helps (98% of clusters opened against an oracle's
0.5% on glove, 61% against 1.1% on BioCLIP), which kills cluster pruning for good.
Sound but unrefereed: Theorem A (union over k, c = 6.0-6.5 at any N) and Theorem B. I see no hole in A, but it
abandons "simultaneous over all items" and forbids certifying by bound alone.
Counts from small samples: survivors against c (40-200 queries, oracle or seed threshold, a global rotated code
that is not v2's code, independent scales per level where the layout needs nested ones).
Modelled and likely optimistic: every QPS for the cascade (380 and 700), built on 0.066 ns per byte, which the
clusters report itself found 40% optimistic here, with a second pass assumed at 5 ns per survivor; v2 is 11-14x
from that kernel. The two kernel forecasts disagree: the ideas report says text 83 ms to 30-45 and item k-NN 4.8
to 1-1.5; the careful integer-kernel design says 83.5 to about 50 and 4.8 to 4.3. Believe the second. Also
modelled: 10,000 QPS approximate on glove, YFCC 11-31k QPS (rests on batching queries by tag, which a database
serving one viewer at a time cannot do), the 25-40% text savings (stand-ins only; the text embeddings are gone
from disk), and everything said about 10M or more.

Three numbers to measure before anyone believes the story:
 a. Native wall time of the certified cascade on all 10,000 glove and nytimes queries, out of cache, against a
    tuned flat scan and against HNSW at recall 0.999 (not 0.99), with the price of certainty stated as the ratio
    to the same scan at m = 3.
 b. Real text queries on BioCLIP at 1M and 10M with the data-only profile: ms against a tuned flat int8 or 1-bit
    scan with re-rank, and the V at which the index overtakes the visible scan.
 c. 100M under a memory cap, deep levels and vectors on NVMe: p50 and p99 latency, pages read per query, resident
    bytes per item.

3. IS "EXACT WITH PROBABILITY 1 - EPS" WORTH ANYTHING AGAINST RECALL 0.999?

For relevance, no. The answer is exact with respect to stored f16 values of an embedding whose own error is
orders larger; 1e-9 is below the chance of a bug or a bit flip. Nobody ranks better at 1.0 than at 0.999.
What has value is three properties that happen to come with it, and two of them do not need exactness:
- Each item's decision depends only on its own data. This is what access control needs (no leak through other
  items, a masked result equals the result over the visible set). A fixed-dial approximate scan has it too.
  The security argument is for item-local decisions, not for eps = 1e-9.
- A per-query guarantee instead of an average. Recall 0.999 is a mean over a benchmark; under filters and for
  out-of-distribution queries the same graph gives 0.3-0.95 with silently short lists and no warning.
- Set semantics. sim(q, x) >= t becomes a predicate: results nest as t moves, counts add across shards, the
  answer survives compaction, inserts of other items and a rebuild. No ANN index returns a threshold set of
  10^5 to 10^7 items at all.
Who pays for that: counts and densities over a similarity filter, joins and near-duplicate sets, diffs over time,
audit and legal holds (defensible because the set is defined by a predicate, not because similarity is truth),
science that must reproduce, and ground truth for other indexes. A map viewer looking at a highlight does not.
Honest pitch: "a similarity predicate with database semantics: threshold and top-k computed exactly inside any
filter, at a cost proportional to the filtered set and several times below scanning it, in about 100 B per item
of RAM." Not "a faster vector index", and not "billions, interactive, unfiltered".

4. DO TEXT QUERIES UNDERMINE IT?

They undermine the elaborate design, not the core. For text the local bases, escape code, support table and
sorted range buy 0-6% (DESIGN section 5) and cost fixed work (tables 13 ms, basis 7 ms of 83). 59% of items are
touched against 10.6%; the session's own report says touching is not closable from data and the profile route is
exhausted. Measured, text is 2.5x flat int8 for k-NN and level with it for thresholds. So for text the project
reduces to a flat certified scan of about one bit per dimension over the visible set: 96 B per item at 768
dimensions, bandwidth-bound at roughly 2-7 ns per item, 2 s or more at 1e9. That is RaBitQ with a rigorous tail
and a constant that does not grow with N. It is worth having, it is small, and it is not six agents of structure.
If text really is the common case, the item-query results (the 18-36x) are the side show, and they are the only
large measured win. Also: no real text query was measured this session.

5. CHEAPEST SEQUENCE TO KILL OR PROVE IT

 0. (no code) Get the distribution of V for the product: visible set times usual filters, and the latency a
    similarity filter may take. Kill the index for the product if most queries have V < 100k (scan wins) or
    V > 50M with an interactive budget (nothing exact wins); keep brute force and an approximate dial.
 1. (1 day) Fair baselines, once: pre-filter plus tuned scan (int8 with exact re-rank, and float BLAS); hnswlib
    with a native filter; ACORN or filtered DiskANN; HNSW pushed to 0.999, including text. Kill the k-NN story
    if a native filtered graph holds 0.999 at 1-10% selectivity at 10x our speed.
 2. (1 day) Re-encode the text queries; run v2 with the data-only profile. Kill the level stream for text if
    k-NN is under 2x a tuned flat int8 scan with re-rank at 1M on one thread.
 3. (1 day, in parallel) Referee Theorems A and B and the self-bounding threshold scheme. If A falls, c returns
    to 7.3-8.6, survivors roughly double, and step 4's bar gets harder.
 4. (3 days) Native 1-bit level and the cascade, end to end, 10,000 queries each on glove, nytimes and BioCLIP
    text. Kill exact k-NN as a product if it is under 3x the flat scan on any of the three, or if the first pass
    is over 2 ns per item on glove or 8 ns at 768 dimensions out of cache.
 5. (1 week) 100M BioCLIP under a 16 GB cap. Kill "billions on one machine" if item p99 exceeds 1 s or pages
    read exceed 10x the byte model; the claim becomes "tens of millions per machine".
 Only after 4 and 5 pass: the write path, then thresholds with the self-bounding union.

6. WHAT TO STOP

- Modelled QPS. No further figure that multiplies a count by an assumed kernel rate.
- "Percentage of the dataset read" as the headline. Report wall time against a tuned scan of the visible set,
  and pages when on disk.
- The approximate mode and ANN-Benchmarks chasing (balanced k-means, SOAR, graphs over centres, stopping rules).
  That is rebuilding ScaNN against strong incumbents and says nothing about the project's point.
- The YFCC track. Its own design says the level stream is not the tool; the winners are filter-first brute
  force, which is the competitor's argument. The L2 and uint8 ports go with it.
- Cluster-level bounds, more anchors, finer clusters. Measured dead.
- Closing the text gap from data alone, profile tuning, stand-in queries.
- Tightening the local-basis code toward its floor (1.36-1.42x) before the kernel is 10x faster.
- Comparing against hnswlib through a Python callback.
- Growing two code families, two metrics and two modes at once. Pick the 1-bit certified level first; add local
  bases back only where item queries on structured data are shown to be the workload.
